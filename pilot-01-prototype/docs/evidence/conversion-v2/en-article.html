    This article is intentionally narrow. It does not rank products by price, predict token performance, claim regulatory approval beyond the cited wording, or tell the reader that any exchange, API, wallet, stablecoin rail, tokenized-asset route or compliance framework is universally safe.     1. OKX says its Third Party App IP Whitelist limits API key usage to whitelisted broker servers when the broker supports the feature.

    4. OKX's API Guide says API keys can be bound to IP addresses and trade or withdraw keys not linked to IP addresses may expire after 14 days of inactivity.

    The source map separates first-party operational documentation from institutional analysis. A venue help page can prove what the venue says about account opening, API behavior, transfer status, whitelists, withdrawal controls or supported routes. An institutional source can prove a policy frame or market-structure concern. Neither type proves a user's private account state, a future fee, a future recovery outcome, a future approval, or a guarantee that support will resolve an error.

    How to Use This Checklist

    Start with the exact product type. A brokerage stock order, a WebSocket connection, a withdrawal whitelist, a tokenized-fund shareclass, a reimbursement program, a security hold and an account-opening flow are different operational objects. If the source uses familiar words such as account, address, order, transfer, approval, finality, verification, coverage or support, still check the mechanism underneath. The mechanism decides who can act, who can reverse or review a problem, what evidence is required, and what remains outside the user's control.

    Second, verify the route. A reader should name the exact official page, exact product surface, exact chain or network, exact account or wallet type and exact action under review. If the evidence cannot be tied to those specifics, the content remains educational context rather than a publishable operational claim. This protects the article from overreaching: it is better to say unknown than to turn a help-page sentence into a guarantee.

    Third, separate convenience from obligation. A smoother interface, faster transfer, lower apparent friction, more familiar brand or more complete policy framework does not create legal rights by itself. Recovery, whitelisting, reimbursement, redemption, finality, account approval, order execution, API availability and regulatory supervision depend on documented obligations and current product controls. A reader should be able to take the checklist and decide what to inspect next without being pushed into an account, deposit, transfer, trade or integration.

    Control Notes

    1. Broker support

    Broker support is the part of the decision where the reader must turn a public source into an operating rule. In this package, it means the IP whitelist flow depends on whether the third-party app or broker participates. The useful action is to confirm support in the OKX API creation flow before sharing any permission. The unresolved edge is whether a specific broker has changed its server IP list after publication, so this article does not convert that uncertainty into a promise, ranking, reward, approval, safety claim or future-availability claim.

    For an operator, broker support should be recorded before funds, account access, wallet recovery, API order flow, whitelist changes, cross-chain transfers or tokenized-asset decisions become material. The record can stay simple: the official source URL, the date checked, the product or network under review, and a non-secret note describing why the next step is acceptable. Screenshots should avoid balances, identity documents, API keys, recovery phrases, private keys, passphrases, verification codes and support-ticket private data.

    FAQ

    Is this article investment advice?

    No. It is a source-mapped operational and decision checklist. It does not recommend buying, selling, staking, depositing, withdrawing, migrating, holding, borrowing against or tokenizing any asset.

    Can a reader rely on an old screenshot or local draft?

    No. The cited source URLs and the review date are the evidence boundary. Local drafts, generated queues and historical notes are not proof that a platform, API, network, account flow, transfer rail or policy state is current.

    What should be checked before acting?

    Check the official URL, product type, jurisdiction or account scope, recovery route, network or settlement rail, support path, rate limit or transfer status, and whether any action exposes credentials, recovery phrases, private keys, API secrets, personal identity documents or balances.

    What should remain unpublished?

    Do not publish secret material, credential fields, identity documents, support ticket private data, personal balances, seed phrases, hidden affiliate promises, unverified rewards, price predictions, fake licensing claims, fake traffic claims or indexing claims.

    Practical Takeaway

    The safest version of this article is the one that stays boring and exact. OKX's third-party app IP whitelist should be read as a broker-connection security control, not as a general endorsement of any app. The useful decision is to verify whether the selected broker supports OKX server IP whitelisting, whether the API key is created for binding third-party apps, and whether the broader API Agreement duties on minimum permissions, MFA, encrypted storage and rotation are satisfied. The user outcome is a clearer next inspection step, not a promise that a platform, API, transfer route or tokenized asset will work under every future condition.
