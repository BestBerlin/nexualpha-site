# NexuAlpha Region Compliance Notes v1

Prepared 2026-10-08. Research only; no site code changed.

## 0. Read this first

- **I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** It is a research summary for the owner to take to qualified counsel. Items marked **[COUNSEL]** need a lawyer in that jurisdiction before any referral CTA goes live there. Items marked **[VERIFY]** are facts I could not confirm from a primary source in this session.
- **Source quality varies.** "Primary" = the regulator's, the exchange's, or the statute's own page that I fetched. "Secondary" = law-firm or news summary. "Headline only" = I saw only a search-result title and did not read the article. Each region cites what it relies on. Several exchange pages were JS shells or 404s when fetched; those exchanges are marked unverified rather than guessed.
- **Exchange restriction lists change without notice** (every list I read says so). Treat everything below as dated 2026-10-08 and re-check before publishing; show a "checked on" date on-page.
- **Core principle:** NexuAlpha's business is paid referral. The safest design is (1) disclose the commission next to every CTA, (2) never show a sign-up CTA for an exchange that publishes a restriction on the visitor's location, (3) in regions where promotion itself is regulated or barred, show information only, no CTA.

## 1. Global baseline (applies to every page and region)

### Must
1. **Commission disclosure adjacent to every CTA**, in the page language, in plain words, visible without clicking or expanding. Example: "NexuAlpha earns a commission if you open an account through this link." US basis: 16 CFR 255.5 says a material connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously when the audience would not reasonably expect it, affiliate-link compensation is a named example, and a disclosure behind a "more" link or only on a profile/about page can fall short (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255). FTC influencer guidance: use plain terms such as "ad", "advertisement", "sponsored"; place the disclosure with the endorsement; do not rely on a platform tool alone (https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers). A footer-only or methodology-page-only disclosure is not enough.
2. **Say that offers vary** by country and change; show the date the offer or fee was checked and link the exchange's official page.
3. **Risk statement** near any CTA: crypto-assets are volatile, you can lose all of the money you put in, and past performance does not predict future results.
4. **State what is and is not verified.** NexuAlpha does not hold exchange licences; do not imply it does.
5. **Honest ranking.** If commission influences order or featuring, say so; link the methodology page (`/methodology` exists).
6. **Record-keeping.** Keep a dated evidence file per exchange per region (source URL, screenshot, checked-on date, who checked) so a regulator or the exchange can be shown why a CTA was or was not displayed.

### Must not (the do-not list)
1. No promise, implication or example that suggests returns, profit, "passive income", "easy money" or "beat the market". No guaranteed or risk-free language.
2. **No fake scarcity or urgency**: no countdown timers, "limited spots", "bonus ends tonight", "last chance" unless the exchange's own published terms state a real end date, which must be cited and dated. FOMO appeals are explicitly prohibited in UK ASA guidance (https://www.asa.org.uk/news/enforcement-notice-advertising-of-cryptocurrencies.html) and in Dubai VARA's marketing rules (secondary: https://www.merklescience.com/blog/uae-varas-new-marketing-regulations-for-virtual-assets).
3. **No hiding or minimising the commission** (no "disclosure" in grey 9px text, no wording like "partner" alone, no placing it below the fold).
4. No bonus amounts, fee discounts or "up to" figures that were not read from the exchange's current official page and dated. Do not copy referral codes/bonuses from third-party pages (my probes showed those are mostly thin AI/UGC pages).
5. No implying an exchange is regulated, licensed, approved, safe or "endorsed" unless a named regulator's register entry is cited. No implying regulator approval of NexuAlpha.
6. **No circumvention guidance**: never suggest VPNs, alternate entities, or false residence/KYC to get around a restriction. "No KYC" content (`/topics/no-kyc` exists) must be informational and must not promote evasion of identity or sanctions rules **[COUNSEL]**.
7. No fabricated testimonials, reviews, user counts or "I made X" stories. No targeting minors.
8. No absolute legal claims ("legal in X"). Use "according to {Exchange}'s published terms as of {date}" and "authorities in X say Y".
9. No leverage/derivatives promotion framed as accessible or easy; derivatives are barred or limited for some users in some regions (examples: OKX derivatives restricted in the UK and Brazil per its page; Binance was barred from derivatives in Brazil by the CVM in 2020).

## 4. Gating logic to implement later (spec only)

1. Visitor picks country (do not rely on IP alone; persist choice). Unknown country defaults to "information only".
2. Data file per exchange: `restricted: [ISO codes]`, `product_limits`, `source_url`, `checked_on`, `reviewer`. CTA component renders only when country is not restricted AND region policy (section 2) allows CTAs AND `checked_on` is within 30 days. Otherwise show the matching gate copy.
3. Region policy table: US = conditional; UK/TR/VN/ID/IN/UAE/ES = info-only by default; EU/BR = conditional on verified authorisation status.
4. Render the disclosure block as part of the CTA component so it cannot be removed per page.
5. Weekly re-check job against exchange pages; alert on list change.
